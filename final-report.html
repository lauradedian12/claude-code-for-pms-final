Release 4.2:

Four responders stopped getting pings after 4.2. Here is what happened, how I found it, what I would build, and the check that stops a brief like this going out half-finished.

Scroll, or use ← → to move between slides.

1 · What happened

Three changes shipped at once

On 12 Aug, release 4.2 changed three things together. Since then fewer pings are taken, and more handlers complain.

1

Ping wait: 90 s → 60 s

Less time to answer before a ping counts as missed.

2

Proximity counts more

Ranking now weighs travel time over a responder’s recent acceptance history (0.45 → 0.60).

3

Console filters persist

Not the cause. Shipped at the same time, so it confuses the picture.

In one sentence

Responders who used to say yes began missing pings. A miss lowers their rank, and a lower rank means fewer chances to earn it back, so some of them went quiet and stayed quiet. That is the likely mechanism, not yet proven: the shorter wait and the heavier proximity weighting can’t be separated until ping response times arrive.

4 · Why it happens

A score you can’t climb back up

Pings go to one responder at a time, best-ranked first. Their score is a running tally, not a rate.

A yes earns
+0.08
A decline or a miss costs
−0.12
Break-even
~60% yes
No decay (a 2019 TODO). Held in memory only.

Scores I modelled from the ping log are estimates. Wen Li’s real scores are still to come.

6 · The fix

Give them a way back

1

Handler can see it

A flag says “No pings in 6 days” and why.

2

A miss isn’t a “no”

Assumed cost: 0.06, half a decline.

3

A way back

After 7 quiet days: a catch-up ping with the full wait. Scores drift up 0.02 a day to 0.5.

4

A late tap counts

If nobody has taken the callout, a late tap takes it.

Try it: click the red flag on Meteor Mite, press “Send the signal”, then take the ping on the phone or skip ahead to see a miss. Items 2 and 3 rest on my assumptions until Wen Li confirms. This prototype doesn’t show the late tap or the fading score yet. The 60 s wait stays until we have response times.

7 · Keeping it from happening again

The same six checks, every brief

I turned my own review habit into a skill. Point it at any brief: /review-checklist path/to/brief.md

Names who owns it
Says how we’ll know it worked
Scope at the end matches the start
Explains the problem before the fix
Success metrics with baselines or placeholders
Clear open questions

It already caught things

Not ready→Ready to go on

Gaps found: no named owner, pilot covered 2 of 4 responders, tickets had no baseline, open questions had no owners. All fixed. A peer’s brief came back “Fix first” for missing baselines on two metrics.

The ask for Helen

Agree the direction and the assumptions before anyone touches the code.

Pilot: four weeks on all four quiet responders, with The Gale as the load comparison. Aim: pings from 3 a week to about 32 combined, missed-ping rate from 12.7% to under 5%, fill rate held at 94% or above.

Watch-out: Supply reads the availability record Dispatch writes and schedules servicing into low-callout windows. Giving pings back to these four changes their load, and Supply sees it with no change on their side.
